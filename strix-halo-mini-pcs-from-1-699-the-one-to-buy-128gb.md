## The chip and the memory

- Ryzen AI Max+ 395: 16 cores, Radeon 8060S, up to 128GB LPDDR5X shared memory, about 256 GB/s.
  [AMD specifications](https://www.amd.com/en/products/processors/laptop/ryzen/ai-300-series/amd-ryzen-ai-max-plus-395.html)
- Up to 96GB of the 128GB can be allocated to graphics (Bosgame's FAQ: "If the system memory is
  128GB, a maximum of 96GB can be allocated").
  [Bosgame store](https://www.bosgamepc.com/products/bosgame-m5-ai-mini-desktop-ryzen-ai-max-395)

## Machines

- Bosgame M5: "Even under full load, the system remains comparatively cool and quiet"; cons include
  "poorly adjustable fan speed"; 2 × USB4, Wi-Fi 7. The store lists two M.2 PCIe 4.0 slots and a 2.5Gbps
  LAN port. [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Best-mini-PC-of-the-year-AMD-Strix-Halo-128-GB-RAM-Radeon-RX-8060S-reviewed-in-the-Bosgame-M5.1087793.0.html)
- GMKtec EVO-X2: "Fan noise proved louder than expected on this mini PC, with the fans becoming
  audible across my living room while running our CPU benchmarks"; a P-mode button "switches between
  three performance modes"; front USB4, two USB-A, SD card reader; 2.5 Gbps Ethernet; CPU/GPU and RAM
  soldered; tricky interior access; 230W external power brick; one-year warranty.
  [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/desktops/mini-pcs/gmktec-evo-x2-ai-mini-pc-review)
- EVO-X2 power: GMKtec lists the chip as stable at 120W with peaks up to 140W.
  [GMKtec](https://www.gmktec.com/products/amd-ryzen%E2%84%A2-ai-max-395-evo-x2-ai-mini-pc)
- Framework Desktop: 5Gbit Ethernet (Realtek RTL8126), 1 year limited warranty, Noctua NF-A12x25 fan
  option. [Framework specs](https://frame.work/desktop?tab=specs)
- Framework Desktop noise, 128GB unit: with all fans forced to 99-100% "there was a persistent and
  clear noise ... but still not as loud as larger desktops. It was quiet enough to still work in a
  typical office/desktop environment". [Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/review/framework-desktop-power)

## Corrected before recording

The supplied script was checked against these pages before the voice was recorded, and four lines
changed:
- A Bosgame "seven day return window" was removed: Bosgame's policy is 30-day returns, with 7 days only
  for units dead on arrival.
- "Gemma 3 70B" became "a seventy billion model": StorageReview names sizes only, and Gemma 3 has no
  70B.
- Framework's per-GB figure is $26.95 ($3,449 / 128), not $26.90.
- "HP's store page has no usable price" was removed: it lists the 128GB/4TB at $5,999.

### Not checked

- Bosgame's "120W steady, 140W peak" power limits are the narration's comparison; GMKtec's own figures
  are sourced above, Bosgame's were not read on its page.
- Framework's $3,449 and $135 were read on a configurator that redirects this machine to its UK store
  (same figures in pounds); the $3,449 US price is confirmed by press reports, the $135 is not.
- The visorcraft log is one contributor's setup; newer drivers and runtimes may change those rates.
